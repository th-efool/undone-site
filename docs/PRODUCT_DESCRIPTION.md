# Undone — Detailed Product Description

Undone is being developed around a fundamental idea: people should be able to make meaningful progress on things they want to learn without turning learning into another project they have to manage.

The central problem is not a lack of curiosity. People already have more things they want to understand than they have time to explore. They save videos, bookmark articles, collect courses, follow tutorials, start books, listen to podcasts, and discover new subjects every day. Yet much of that curiosity remains unfinished. Interests accumulate faster than understanding, and the effort required to organize, resume, and continue learning becomes another obstacle.

Undone is intended to address the gap between wanting to learn something and actually building lasting understanding of it.

Instead of requiring people to plan their learning, decide what to study next, reconstruct where they left off, or maintain a collection of disconnected resources, the product aims to make learning something they can naturally return to in the spare moments of everyday life.

The experience should feel less like managing a course and more like opening an app, finding something interesting, and continuing to learn immediately.

## 1. The problem Undone is solving

The internet has made knowledge abundant, but it has not made learning easy to sustain.

Someone can discover a 20-hour programming course, save a collection of psychology videos, bookmark articles about artificial intelligence, and begin learning a new language—all within a single week. Every resource represents genuine interest. The difficulty begins when those interests have to coexist with work, college, responsibilities, changing schedules, and limited attention.

Over time, the collection becomes a backlog of unfinished intentions.

- You save something because it interests you, but never return to it.
- You start a long course, lose momentum, and struggle to resume.
- You have several subjects you genuinely want to understand but cannot decide which deserves attention.
- You return to a resource after weeks and have forgotten the context.
- You spend time organizing what you want to learn instead of actually learning it.

The underlying problem is coordination overhead. Conventional self-directed learning makes the learner responsible for selecting resources, breaking them down, establishing a routine, tracking progress, and deciding what comes next.

Undone is designed to absorb that overhead.

Its purpose is not to help people consume more information. It is to help them turn existing curiosity into sustained learning and eventual understanding.

## 2. How Undone works

### 1. Bring what you want to learn

Start with something that already interests you: a YouTube playlist, a long-form video, a collection of articles, a blog, a tutorial series, or other supported learning material. You do not need to begin with a predefined course created by the platform.

### 2. The material becomes manageable

Undone processes the supplied material and organizes it into smaller, meaningful learning segments. A resource that originally demands hours of uninterrupted attention can become something approachable across multiple short sessions.

### 3. Learning fits into ordinary life

Instead of requiring a dedicated study block every day, the experience accommodates irregular pockets of free time. Five minutes now, fifteen minutes later, and another short session tomorrow can all contribute to the same larger objective.

### 4. Progress continues automatically

The system is intended to preserve context, adapt pacing, and determine the next useful learning step. When you return, you should not have to reconstruct your plan or remember where you stopped. You simply continue.

The critical distinction is that dividing content into smaller pieces is only the beginning. Undone is intended to connect those pieces into an ongoing process, so that individual sessions contribute to a larger outcome rather than remaining isolated moments of consumption.

## 3. The experience: learning without managing learning

The defining product principle is that the complexity should live inside the system, not in the user's daily experience.

Traditional learning applications often present a dashboard, a list of unfinished lessons, a progress tracker, a calendar, or a collection of tasks. These tools can help organize learning, but they also repeatedly ask the learner to make decisions before doing the actual work.

Undone aims for a different interaction model.

You open the app because you have a few minutes. The system already has the relevant context. Rather than asking you to choose between everything you have started, it presents a meaningful next step.

You begin learning immediately.

As you continue, the system maintains the relationship between the current material and the broader subject. Where appropriate, the experience can incorporate reinforcement, recall, and connections between concepts rather than treating every resource as a disconnected sequence of videos.

When your available time changes, the pace should adjust. When you return after an interruption, your progress should remain intact. When you have multiple interests, those interests should be able to coexist without requiring you to manually coordinate every learning session.

The intended loop is simple:

### Open
Start with the time you have.

### Learn
Engage with the next meaningful idea.

### Return
Pick up without rebuilding context.

### Understand
Individual sessions accumulate into real progress.

This is not about making every learning session shorter for its own sake. It is about making the larger commitment easier to sustain, even when life does not provide a predictable schedule.

## 4. Multiple interests, one continuous experience

Undone is designed for people whose curiosity does not follow a single linear path.

Someone might be learning systems programming, exploring cognitive psychology, studying Japanese, and following an artificial intelligence research series at the same time. These interests do not necessarily compete because the person lacks commitment. They compete because conventional learning systems often assume that one subject must take priority until it is completed.

Undone's intended approach is to accommodate multiple parallel pursuits.

Each subject can maintain its own context, progression, and learning requirements while the overall experience helps determine what should appear next. A session might continue a technical topic, revisit an earlier concept that needs reinforcement, or return to another interest that has been waiting.

The objective is not to turn every interest into another obligation. It is to let interests coexist without making the learner manage a separate system for each one.

This is what makes structured curiosity a useful description of the product: curiosity remains open-ended, while the process of developing deeper understanding becomes continuous.

## 5. Cohorts: learning alongside other people

Undone is also intended to support shared learning through cohorts.

A cohort is a group organized around a common learning subject or set of resources. Someone interested in a topic can create a cohort, keep it private, or make it available for others to join. Alternatively, they can discover an existing cohort rather than building one from scratch.

The community layer can support shared notes, progress visibility, reminders, and lightweight nudges between participants. Public cohorts can also be evaluated using indicators such as activity and completion, helping prospective members distinguish active learning groups from abandoned ones.

The value is not simply adding a social network to an educational product. It is making continued participation less solitary.

### Shared direction
Participants are working toward a common subject or outcome, even when they study at different times.

### Lightweight accountability
Members can encourage one another to return without turning learning into a competitive obligation.

### Collective progress
Shared context and participation can make it easier to stay connected to the subject and the people learning it.

The intended result is a system in which individual learning remains personal, while community provides additional continuity and motivation.

## 6. What makes Undone different?

The distinction becomes clearer when comparing the jobs performed by familiar digital tools.

| Existing tool | What it primarily provides | What Undone aims to add |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bookmarks and Watch Later | A place to save material | A way to continue through it |
| Video playlists | An ordered collection of content | Smaller, more manageable learning segments |
| Online courses | A predefined curriculum | A process built around material the learner already cares about |
| Productivity apps | Tools to plan and track work | Less need to manage learning manually |
| AI summarizers | Condensed information | Ongoing progression toward deeper understanding |
| Learning communities | Shared discussion and participation | Shared learning organized around continuing through material |

Undone is not differentiated merely by accepting links or breaking videos into chunks. Those are individual capabilities. The more fundamental proposition is that the system connects material, pacing, continuity, and learning progression into one experience.

Most tools help with one part of the process. Undone aims to handle the transition from interest to sustained progress.

Its intended optimization target is also different from that of conventional social feeds. A typical content feed benefits when users continue consuming. Undone should instead help users develop competence and make meaningful progress toward completing what they set out to learn.

## 7. Who is Undone for?

Undone is intended for curious people who want to learn more than their schedules and existing tools allow them to sustain.

### The skill builder
Someone learning a technical skill, a new language, or a subject that could lead to a career change or personal project. They need long-term progress without requiring every session to be a major time commitment.

### The curious explorer
Someone who constantly discovers fascinating topics, saves resources, and begins new rabbit holes, but struggles to turn all that interest into sustained understanding.

### The time-constrained learner
Someone who wants to make meaningful progress around work, college, family, and other responsibilities, without depending on uninterrupted hours.

### The inconsistent learner
Someone who starts with genuine enthusiasm but loses momentum when life becomes busy. They need a way to return without feeling that every interruption has undone their previous effort.

These are different expressions of the same underlying problem: the desire to learn is present, but the process required to sustain it repeatedly breaks down.

Undone is not primarily defined by age, academic status, or a particular profession. Its defining audience is people who have things they genuinely want to understand and need a better way to keep going.

## 8. The role of intelligence and automation

The product's intelligence is intended to operate largely behind the scenes.

At a conceptual level, this means maintaining a representation of the learning material and its relationships, preserving the learner's progress, adapting session size and pacing, and determining what should come next.

As the system develops, that may involve:

- **Content understanding**: identifying meaningful segments within supplied resources.
- **Progress tracking**: maintaining the relationship between completed material and what remains.
- **Adaptive pacing**: adjusting the learning process to available time and changing circumstances.
- **Context preservation**: retaining the relevant state so that interruptions do not force a restart.
- **Knowledge reinforcement**: revisiting concepts when additional practice or recall would be useful.
- **Interest coordination**: balancing multiple ongoing learning pursuits.
- **Cohort intelligence**: helping people discover and participate in shared learning experiences.

These describe the intended product capabilities, not a claim that every capability is already implemented or that every supported resource can be processed today.

The guiding principle is that better internal intelligence should result in less visible complexity. Users should not need to understand learning graphs, scheduling algorithms, recommendation logic, or memory models to benefit from them.

## 9. What Undone ultimately represents

At its deepest level, Undone is about changing the relationship between curiosity and effort.

Today, discovering something interesting often creates another item on an already crowded list. What begins as excitement can gradually become an obligation: another video to finish, another course to resume, another subject to get back to someday.

Undone aims to remove that sense of accumulating responsibility. It gives existing curiosity a process through which it can develop, without requiring the learner to become an expert at planning, organizing, and maintaining their own education.

The ambition is that a person can spend a few minutes learning today, return when they have time tomorrow, explore another interest when they feel curious, and continue building understanding over weeks or months.

The learner does not need to become more disciplined simply to overcome the organizational burden of learning. The system should take on more of that burden itself.

That is the central idea behind Undone:

The internet made it easy to discover almost anything. Undone aims to make it easier to actually learn it.

Not more things saved for later. Not more information consumed without direction. Not another productivity system to maintain.

A way to keep following your curiosity until it becomes understanding.
